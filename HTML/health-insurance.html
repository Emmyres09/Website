Project Overview

State-by-state health insurance coverage analysis by Medicaid expansion.

This Excel dashboard analyzes health insurance data from Quantum Analytics NG, comparing coverage metrics between states with and without Medicaid expansion. Pivot tables and slicers enable granular analysis across all 50 states and DC.

Healthcare policymakers, insurers, and analysts can benchmark coverage rates, identify expansion impact gaps, and understand state-level disparities in insurance access and affordability.

Data & Tools

State-level health insurance coverage and Medicaid data.

  • Data source: Quantum Analytics NG – Comprehensive health insurance dataset with state-level enrollment, premiums, claims, and Medicaid expansion status.
  • Grain: State-level records including insured population %, Medicaid enrollment, private insurance rates, and expansion policy status.
  • Excel features: State slicer, Medicaid Expansion slicer, pivot tables with calculated fields, conditional formatting, and dynamic charts.

Slicer Setup

State and Medicaid Expansion filtering controls.

Dual slicers enable precise comparison between expansion and non-expansion states at national, regional, and individual state levels.

  • State: Filter all 50 states + DC for granular geographic analysis.
  • Medicaid Expansion: Compare 40 expansion states vs 10 non-expansion states.
  • Cross-filtering: Regional analysis (Northeast, South, Midwest, West) by expansion status.

Answers questions like "California vs Texas coverage rates" or "Do expansion states have higher insured rates?"

Expansion Success States

States showing strongest coverage improvements.

  • Best performers: Highest % point drops in uninsured rates post-expansion.
  • High Medicaid growth: Largest enrollment increases in expansion states.
  • Affordability wins: States with lowest premium-to-income ratios.

Benchmark states for successful policy implementation.

Coverage Gaps

States needing policy attention.

  • Highest uninsured: Non-expansion states with persistent coverage gaps.
  • Premium burden: States where insurance costs exceed affordability thresholds.
  • Low utilization: States with high coverage but low healthcare access.

Priority areas for federal and state intervention.
